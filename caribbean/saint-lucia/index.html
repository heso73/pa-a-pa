Starting a business in Saint Lucia: taxes, registration and steps

A plain-words guide for first-time entrepreneurs in Saint Lucia: what you register, what you pay and when. Figures are 2026 estimates for planning. Inland Revenue Department (IRD) gives you the exact amounts when you register.

Steps to start

  1. Register with the Inland Revenue and get your tax number (TAN)
    A number that identifies you to the tax office. You need it for almost every other step.
  2. Choose your business name and check that it is free
    If you trade under any name other than your own, you must register it. Search the name at the ROCIP first.
  3. Register the business name, or incorporate the company, with the Registry of Companies and Intellectual Property (ROCIP)
    A sole trader registers the business name. A company must be incorporated first.
  4. Register with the National Insurance Corporation (NIC)
    Self-employed people must register and contribute. It gives access to a pension and other benefits.
  5. Open a separate bank account for the business
    Keeping business money apart from household money makes your numbers clearer and your taxes easier to pay.
  6. Register as an employer and prepare monthly payments (if you hire employees)
    With employees you pay social security (and income tax withheld, where it applies) every month. The authorities give you your payment dates when you register as an employer.
  7. Register for VAT (when your sales reach the threshold)
    Required once your yearly sales reach the VAT threshold (EC$180,000). Register with the Inland Revenue.

Choosing a legal form

  • Registered business name: You run the business as an individual under your own name or a business name. You and the business are the same person for tax. Usually the simplest way to start.
  • Limited company: The company is its own legal person, separate from you. It costs more to set up and has more paperwork, but it can limit your personal risk. A company costs more to set up than a business name. The registry will quote you the current fees.

Taxes and contributions

  • Income tax: 15% on the first EC$15,000 of chargeable income, 20% up to EC$30,000, then 30% (personal allowance per year).
  • National Insurance (NIC) (10%): National insurance gives access to a pension and other benefits. A self-employed person pays both shares: about 10% of earnings up to EC$5,000 a month.
  • Value Added Tax (VAT): 12.5%. Plan to register within 10 days once your sales reach EC$180,000, the level published by the government. The IRD gives you the exact level when you register. VAT returns are filed monthly.

Tax calendar

  • March 25, June 25 and September 25: Pay your advance income tax instalment. The balance is paid with your return.
  • By March 31: File your annual income tax return for the year before.
  • Every month: Every month: pay social security (and income tax withheld, where it applies) for the previous month.

Frequently asked questions

How much tax does a self-employed person pay in Saint Lucia?

On profit: 15% on the first EC$15,000 of chargeable income, 20% up to EC$30,000, then 30% (personal allowance per year). Contributions: National Insurance (NIC) 10% (up to EC$5,000 a month). Example: with EC$12,000 of sales a month, about EC$4,288 a month is left after tax.

Do I need to register for VAT in Saint Lucia?

Value Added Tax (VAT) 12.5%; register from EC$180,000 in yearly sales. Plan to register within 10 days once your sales reach EC$180,000, the level published by the government. The IRD gives you the exact level when you register. VAT returns are filed monthly.

Where do I register a business in Saint Lucia?

With Registry of Companies and Intellectual Property (ROCIP) and Inland Revenue Department (IRD).
