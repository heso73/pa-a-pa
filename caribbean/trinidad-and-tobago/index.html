Starting a business in Trinidad and Tobago: taxes, registration and steps

A plain-words guide for first-time entrepreneurs in Trinidad and Tobago: what you register, what you pay and when. Figures are 2026 estimates for planning. Inland Revenue Division (IRD) gives you the exact amounts when you register.

Steps to start

  1. Search and reserve your business or company name
    Two businesses cannot have the same or a similar name. You can search and reserve online through TTBizLink (you need a TTConnect account).
  2. Register the business name, or incorporate the company, with the Companies Registry
    Online through TTBizLink or at the Registry office. A company also needs a registered office in Trinidad and Tobago.
  3. Register with the Inland Revenue Division (BIR file number)
    This is how you file income tax. Self-employed people also pay their tax in advance, each quarter.
  4. Register with the National Insurance Board (NIBTT)
    Self-employed people must register and contribute. It gives you access to a pension and other benefits.
  5. Open a separate bank account for the business
    Keeping business money apart from household money makes your numbers clearer and your taxes easier to pay.
  6. Register as an employer and prepare monthly payments (if you hire employees)
    With employees you pay deductions (income tax, NIS, Health Surcharge) each month. The IRD and NIBTT give you the deadlines when you register.
  7. Register for VAT (when your sales reach the threshold)
    Required when your sales reach TT$500,000 in twelve months, or when you expect them to. Registration is free.

Choosing a legal form

  • Registered business name: You run the business as an individual under your own name or a business name. You and the business are the same person for tax. Usually the simplest way to start. Budget about TT$20 to TT$25 for the name reservation and about TT$200 to TT$220 for the registration.
  • Limited company: The company is its own legal person, separate from you. It costs more to set up and has more paperwork, but it can limit your personal risk. A company costs more to set up than a business name. The registry will quote you the current fees.

Taxes and contributions

  • Income tax: 25% on chargeable income up to TT$1,000,000, then 30% (personal allowance per year).
  • NIS (16.2%): National Insurance. It gives access to a pension and other benefits. Contributions are set by earnings class, so this is an estimate.
  • Health Surcharge: A small fixed weekly amount for public health care (about TT$8.25 a week at most earnings levels).
  • Green Fund Levy (0.3% of gross sales): charged on sales, not on profit.
  • Value Added Tax (VAT): 12.5%. You must also register if you expect to reach it in the next twelve months. Registration is free. Your filing period is set when you register.

Business levy: businesses with gross sales above TT$360,000 pay 0.6% of gross sales if that is more than their income tax. It is not payable in the first three years of a business, and it is not included in your estimate.

Tax calendar

  • March 31, June 30, September 30 and December 15: Pay your advance income tax instalment and your NIS contribution.
  • By April 30: File your income tax return for the year before.
  • Every month: Every month: pay payroll deductions for the previous month (income tax, NIS, Health Surcharge). The IRD and NIBTT give you the exact dates when you register as an employer.

Frequently asked questions

How much tax does a self-employed person pay in Trinidad and Tobago?

On profit: 25% on chargeable income up to TT$1,000,000, then 30% (personal allowance per year). Contributions: NIS 16.2% (up to TT$13,600 a month), Health Surcharge. On sales: Green Fund Levy (0.3% of gross sales) 0.3%. Example: with TT$25,000 of sales a month, about TT$8,895 a month is left after tax.

Do I need to register for VAT in Trinidad and Tobago?

Value Added Tax (VAT) 12.5%; register from TT$500,000 in yearly sales. You must also register if you expect to reach it in the next twelve months. Registration is free. Your filing period is set when you register.

Where do I register a business in Trinidad and Tobago?

With Companies Registry (TTBizLink) and Inland Revenue Division (IRD). First steps: Search and reserve your business or company name; Register the business name, or incorporate the company, with the Companies Registry; Register with the Inland Revenue Division (BIR file number).

How much does it cost to employ someone in Trinidad and Tobago?

On top of gross pay, the employer adds: NIS 10.8%. Every month: pay payroll deductions for the previous month (income tax, NIS, Health Surcharge). The IRD and NIBTT give you the exact dates when you register as an employer.
