Starting a business in British Virgin Islands: taxes, registration and steps

A plain-words guide for first-time entrepreneurs in British Virgin Islands: what you register, what you pay and when. Figures are 2026 estimates for planning. Inland Revenue Department (IRD) gives you the exact amounts when you register.

Steps to start

  1. Choose your business name and check that it is free
    Search the name first.
  2. Get your trade licence
    Most businesses need a licence for their activity from the Department of Trade and Consumer Affairs.
  3. Register with the Inland Revenue Department within 30 days
    This registers you for payroll tax. Self-employed owners pay payroll tax on their own earnings.
  4. Register with the Social Security Board and the National Health Insurance (NHI)
    Self-employed people must contribute to both.
  5. Open a separate bank account for the business
    Keeping business money apart from household money makes your numbers clearer and your taxes easier to pay.
  6. Prepare payroll tax, Social Security and NHI for your employees (if you hire employees)
    With employees you pay all three every month, by the 15th of the following month.

Choosing a legal form

  • Sole trader (registered business): You run the business as an individual under your own name or a business name. You and the business are the same person for tax. Usually the simplest way to start. The Department of Trade and Consumer Affairs quotes the trade licence fee for your activity.
  • Limited company: The company is its own legal person, separate from you. It costs more to set up and has more paperwork, but it can limit your personal risk. A company costs more to set up than a sole trader business. The Registry of Corporate Affairs quotes the current fees.

Taxes and contributions

  • Payroll tax: 10% of earnings above US$10,000 (14% if you employ 7 or more people) (the first US$10,000 a year is exempt).
  • Social Security (8.5%): Social Security gives access to a pension and other benefits. Self-employed people pay 8.5% of their earnings, up to US$53,400 a year.
  • National Health Insurance (NHI) (7.5%): NHI covers health care. Self-employed people pay both shares: 7.5% of their earnings, up to US$106,800 a year.

The British Virgin Islands has no income tax and no sales tax. Self-employed owners pay payroll tax on their own earnings above US$10,000, plus Social Security and NHI.

Payroll tax for a business with fewer than 7 employees: 10% of pay above US$10,000 per employee (8% deducted from the employee, 2% paid by the employer).

Tax calendar

  • Every month: Pay payroll tax, Social Security and NHI for your employees by the 15th of the following month.
  • Every year: File your payroll tax return with the Inland Revenue Department.
  • Every month: Every month: pay payroll tax, Social Security and NHI for the previous month, by the 15th.

Frequently asked questions

How much tax does a self-employed person pay in British Virgin Islands?

On profit: 10% of earnings above US$10,000 (14% if you employ 7 or more people) (the first US$10,000 a year is exempt). Contributions: Social Security 8.5% (up to US$4,450 a month), National Health Insurance (NHI) 7.5% (up to US$8,900 a month). Example: with US$6,000 of sales a month, about US$2,155 a month is left after tax.

Is there a sales tax or VAT in British Virgin Islands?

No. British Virgin Islands has no general sales tax. The costs to plan for are the ones in the taxes and contributions list on this page.

Where do I register a business in British Virgin Islands?

With BVI Financial Services Commission (Registry of Corporate Affairs) and Inland Revenue Department (IRD). First steps: Choose your business name and check that it is free; Get your trade licence; Register with the Inland Revenue Department within 30 days.

How much does it cost to employ someone in British Virgin Islands?

On top of gross pay, the employer adds: Payroll tax (employer share) 2%, Social Security 4.5%, National Health Insurance (NHI) 3.75%. Every month: pay payroll tax, Social Security and NHI for the previous month, by the 15th.
