Starting a business in The Bahamas: taxes, registration and steps

A plain-words guide for first-time entrepreneurs in The Bahamas: what you register, what you pay and when. Figures are 2026 estimates for planning. Department of Inland Revenue gives you the exact amounts when you register.

Steps to start

  1. Choose your business name and register it (a company is incorporated at the Registrar General's Department)
    A sole proprietor does not need to incorporate, but must hold a business licence. A company must be incorporated first.
  2. Get your business licence from the Department of Inland Revenue
    Anyone carrying on business in The Bahamas needs one, even when sales are too low to pay any licence tax. You renew it every year.
  3. Register with the National Insurance Board (NIB)
    Self-employed people should register within 10 working days of starting, and decide at the start of each year the income their contributions are based on.
  4. Open a separate bank account for the business
    Keeping business money apart from household money makes your numbers clearer and your taxes easier to pay.
  5. Register as an employer with NIB (form R.1) and prepare monthly payments (if you hire employees)
    With employees you pay National Insurance each month (form C-10), by the 15th of the following month.
  6. Register for VAT (when your sales reach the threshold)
    Required when your taxable sales reach B$100,000 in twelve months.

Choosing a legal form

  • Sole proprietor: You run the business as an individual, under your own name or a business name. You and the business are the same person. Usually the simplest way to start. You need a business licence from the Department of Inland Revenue. The initial filing fee is about B$100.
  • Limited company: The company is its own legal person, separate from you. It costs more to set up and has more paperwork, but it can limit your personal risk. A company costs more to set up than a business name. The registry will quote you the current fees.

Taxes and contributions

  • National Insurance (NIB) (10.3%): National Insurance gives access to a pension and other benefits. Self-employed people pay 10.3% of their declared insurable income, up to B$810 a week.
  • Business licence tax: charged on sales, not on profit.
  • Value Added Tax (VAT): 10%. Some activities must register whatever their sales. Returns and payment are due within 21 days after each period.

The Bahamas has no income tax on profit. The main costs are National Insurance, VAT (above B$100,000 in sales) and the business licence tax.

Tax calendar

  • Every month: Self-employed: pay your National Insurance contribution. Ask NIB for your payment schedule.
  • By January 31: Renew your business licence and file your turnover return for the year before.
  • Every month: Every month: pay National Insurance for the previous month (form C-10).

Frequently asked questions

How much tax does a self-employed person pay in The Bahamas?

On profit: No personal income tax in The Bahamas. Contributions: National Insurance (NIB) 10.3% (up to B$3,510 a month). On sales: Business licence tax. Example: with B$6,000 of sales a month, about B$2,870 a month is left after tax.

Do I need to register for VAT in The Bahamas?

Value Added Tax (VAT) 10%; register from B$100,000 in yearly sales. Some activities must register whatever their sales. Returns and payment are due within 21 days after each period.

Where do I register a business in The Bahamas?

With Registrar General's Department and Department of Inland Revenue. First steps: Choose your business name and register it (a company is incorporated at the Registrar General's Department); Get your business licence from the Department of Inland Revenue; Register with the National Insurance Board (NIB).

How much does it cost to employ someone in The Bahamas?

On top of gross pay, the employer adds: National Insurance (NIB) 6.65%. Every month: pay National Insurance for the previous month (form C-10).
