Starting a business in Antigua and Barbuda: taxes, registration and steps

A plain-words guide for first-time entrepreneurs in Antigua and Barbuda: what you register, what you pay and when. Figures are 2026 estimates for planning. Inland Revenue Department (IRD) gives you the exact amounts when you register.

Steps to start

  1. Register with the Inland Revenue and get your tax number (TIN)
    A number that identifies you to the tax office. You need it for almost every other step.
  2. Choose your business name and check that it is free
    If you trade under any name other than your own, you must register it. Search the name at the ABIPCO first.
  3. Register the business name, or incorporate the company, with the Antigua and Barbuda Intellectual Property and Commerce Office (ABIPCO)
    A sole trader registers the business name. A company must be incorporated first.
  4. Register with the Antigua and Barbuda Social Security Board
    Self-employed people must register and contribute. It gives access to a pension and other benefits.
  5. Open a separate bank account for the business
    Keeping business money apart from household money makes your numbers clearer and your taxes easier to pay.
  6. Register as an employer and prepare monthly payments (if you hire employees)
    With employees you pay social security (and income tax withheld, where it applies) every month. The authorities give you your payment dates when you register as an employer.
  7. Register for ABST (when your sales reach the threshold)
    Required once your yearly sales reach the ABST threshold (EC$300,000). Register with the Inland Revenue.

Choosing a legal form

  • Registered business name: You run the business as an individual under your own name or a business name. You and the business are the same person for tax. Usually the simplest way to start. The registry will quote you the current registration fee.
  • Limited company: The company is its own legal person, separate from you. It costs more to set up and has more paperwork, but it can limit your personal risk. A company costs more to set up than a business name. The registry will quote you the current fees.

Taxes and contributions

  • Social Security (10%): Social security gives access to a pension and other benefits. Self-employed people pay 10% of their declared earnings, up to EC$6,500 a month.
  • Antigua and Barbuda Sales Tax (ABST): 17%. The rate is 17%, in force since 2024. The IRD gives you the exact registration level when you register. ABST returns are filed monthly.

Antigua and Barbuda has no personal income tax. The Education Levy on pay is not included in your estimate.

Tax calendar

  • Payment dates: Your advance tax payments follow the schedule the Inland Revenue gives you when you register.
  • Each year: File the annual returns that apply to your business. The Inland Revenue Department publishes the deadlines each year.
  • Every month: Every month: pay social security (and income tax withheld, where it applies) for the previous month.

Frequently asked questions

How much tax does a self-employed person pay in Antigua and Barbuda?

On profit: No personal income tax in Antigua and Barbuda. Contributions: Social Security 10% (up to EC$6,500 a month). Example: with EC$12,000 of sales a month, about EC$4,950 a month is left after tax.

Do I need to register for ABST in Antigua and Barbuda?

Antigua and Barbuda Sales Tax (ABST) 17%; register from EC$300,000 in yearly sales. The rate is 17%, in force since 2024. The IRD gives you the exact registration level when you register. ABST returns are filed monthly.

Where do I register a business in Antigua and Barbuda?

With Antigua and Barbuda Intellectual Property and Commerce Office (ABIPCO) and Inland Revenue Department (IRD). First steps: Register with the Inland Revenue and get your tax number (TIN); Choose your business name and check that it is free; Register the business name, or incorporate the company, with the Antigua and Barbuda Intellectual Property and Commerce Office (ABIPCO).

How much does it cost to employ someone in Antigua and Barbuda?

On top of gross pay, the employer adds: Social Security 8.5%, Medical Benefits Scheme 3.5%. Every month: pay social security (and income tax withheld, where it applies) for the previous month.
