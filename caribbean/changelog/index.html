Pa a Pa Caribbean

Changelog

New countries, new languages and data updates, newest first.

2026-10-04: Second pass: Belize

The business tax rates by activity (1.75% for trade, 6% for professional services, 3% for rentals, 6% for tour operators, 19% for telecommunications) are confirmed by the Belize Tax Service and BELTRAIDE, and a note lists the other rates.

2026-10-04: Second pass: Barbados and Guyana

Barbados: the standard corporation tax is 9% since 2024 (5.5% for registered small businesses with gross income up to BDS$2 million), not the old sliding scale; self-employed advance tax payments are due on 15 June, 15 September and 15 December with the balance by 30 April. Guyana: the corporation tax rates (40% commercial, 25% others) and the 2% minimum tax for commercial companies are confirmed.

2026-10-04: Dominica Social Security 2026

Employee contribution corrected to 6.75% (it was 7%) and employer contribution to 8% from January 2026 (it was 7.75%). The self-employed rate stays at 13.75% for 2026 and rises by 0.25 point each January until 2031.

2026-10-04: Second pass: Bahamas, Montserrat, Saint Vincent, Grenada

The Bahamas: the National Insurance weekly ceiling rose from B$810 to B$830 on 1 July 2026 (NIB announcement), and the estimate now uses it. Montserrat: the income tax table (EC$18,000 allowance, then 5%, 20%, 25%, 30% and 40%) is confirmed by the 2024 amendment and the 2026 allowance. Saint Vincent and the Grenadines: the VAT level of EC$300,000 and the 28% top income tax rate are confirmed on the tax office site. Grenada: a note flags that some sources show 15% and 30%.

2026-10-04: New guide: the rules of a profitable business

A new page in the four languages explains in plain words how a business becomes and stays profitable: ten rules (profit, price floor, break-even, paying yourself, cash, safety net, taxes, separate accounts, reinvesting in the right order, monthly measuring), seven mistakes to avoid and a 30-minute monthly routine. Each rule shows the person's own numbers once the test is done.

2026-10-04: Plainer wording for beginners

Abbreviations that were not explained are now spelled out where they appear (tax numbers such as TIN, TRN, EIN and CRIB, social insurance names such as AOV, AWW, ZV and OV, and the Jamaican HEART, NHT and GCT), in English, Spanish and Dutch.

2026-10-04: The Bahamas checked: all 29 countries reviewed

The Bahamas: the National Insurance rates (10.3% for the self-employed, 4.65% employee, 6.65% employer) were confirmed on the NIB site, and the turnover-based business licence tax of the 2023 Act is now explained in a note. This completes a first review of all 29 countries and territories.

2026-10-04: British territories checked

Montserrat: the VAT was removed, because the government's investment agency states that Montserrat has no VAT or similar tax; the income tax table is flagged to verify. Cayman Islands: a note explains the Small Business Incentive Programme, which cancels the trade and business licence fee for micro businesses. Turks and Caicos: business licence fees range from about US$100 to US$7,500 and new legislation was passed in January 2026. Anguilla: the Interim Stabilisation Levy and the 13% GST were confirmed on the tax office site. British Virgin Islands: licence fees depend on the category and on whether you are a BVIslander.

2026-10-04: Aruba and Curaçao checked

Aruba: the 2026 income tax table (tax-free amount Afl. 30,000, then 0% to Afl. 34,930, 21%, 42% and 52%) was confirmed on the Tax Authority site, and a note mentions that the government is studying a switch to VAT. Curaçao: the 6% turnover tax (7% for insurance and short stays, 9% for certain goods) and the profit tax of 15% up to Cg 500,000 and 22% above were confirmed on the Tax Authority site.

2026-10-04: Suriname corrected, Belize checked

Suriname: the income tax thresholds were outdated. For 2024 to 2026 the first SRD 108,000 a year is tax-free, then 8% up to SRD 150,000, 18% up to SRD 192,000, 28% up to SRD 234,000 and 38% above, instead of the old thresholds of a few thousand dollars. The estimate for a typical business is now much lower. Belize: the Social Security Board confirms the self-employed rate of 7% of declared weekly income.

2026-10-04: Grenada and Sint Maarten checked

Grenada: the EC$36,000 personal allowance was confirmed on the Ministry of Finance site and the EC$300,000 VAT level agrees across sources; the top rate (28% or 30%) still differs between an older official page and recent sources. Sint Maarten: the 5% turnover tax and the 34.5% profit tax were reconfirmed; the 2026 income tax table still could not be found.

2026-10-04: Dominican Republic and Puerto Rico checked

Dominican Republic: the 2026 income tax scale (exempt up to RD$416,220, then 15%, 20% and 25%) was confirmed, and the 2027 scale of Law 30-26 is now explained in full (exempt up to RD$480,000; 15%, 20%, 25% and a new 27% band). Puerto Rico: the proposed individual tax reform (Senate Bill 912) is described with its bands; it was not found to be law, so the current table stays.

2026-10-04: Saint Vincent and the Grenadines and Cuba checked

Cuba: the rates for micro, small and medium businesses were confirmed on a Ministry of Finance document (35% profit tax, 10% sales tax on total income, 1% territorial contribution, 5% labour tax, 14% social security), and a note now mentions the tax on profits paid out to partners. Saint Vincent and the Grenadines: the 28% top income tax rate was confirmed; the allowance and the VAT threshold still differ between sources and stay marked to verify.

2026-10-04: Dominica and Saint Lucia checked

Dominica: the VAT registration level was corrected from EC$300,000 to EC$250,000, the level published by the Inland Revenue Division. Saint Lucia: the VAT registration level was corrected from EC$180,000 to EC$400,000, as shown on most pages of the tax office site, and the 2023 income tax table (EC$25,000 allowance, 15%, 20%, 30%) was confirmed on a government document.

2026-10-04: Antigua and Barbuda and Saint Kitts and Nevis checked

Antigua and Barbuda: the EC$300,000 ABST registration level was confirmed on the tax office site, with the early-registration rule and the absence of a registration fee. Saint Kitts and Nevis: the VAT registration level was corrected from EC$400,000 to EC$150,000 for goods (EC$96,000 for certain services) after several sources agreed, and a note now mentions the unincorporated business tax of about 4%.

2026-10-04: Jamaica and Guyana checked

Jamaica: the J$1,902,360 tax-free amount from April 2026 was confirmed and the April 2027 rise to about J$2,003,496 is now noted. Guyana: the NIS site confirms that the self-employed pay 12.5% of declared income; the 14% seen in other sources is the combined rate for employed people.

2026-10-04: Bonaire, Saba and Sint Eustatius checked

The 2026 income tax figures (29.4% up to US$53,198, then 38.4%, tax-free allowance US$21,956) and the ABB rates were confirmed on the Caribbean Netherlands tax office site. New note: sole traders with sales under US$30,000 a year can ask for an ABB exemption, and the ABB is declared every quarter.

2026-10-04: Haiti and US Virgin Islands checked

Haiti: the fixed patente of G 1,250, G 2,500 or G 5,000 by commune group was confirmed on the DGI site, and a note now explains that a new business pays only the fixed G 5,000 in its first period. US Virgin Islands: the US$390 yearly business licence renewal was confirmed on the DLCA site; the 2026 federal-based tax figures were re-read.

2026-10-04: Trinidad and Tobago and Barbados checked

Trinidad and Tobago: the TT$90,000 personal allowance and the 16.2% National Insurance rate for 2026 were confirmed; the rate is reported to rise to 19.2% in January 2027. Barbados: the VAT threshold rise to BDS$350,000 from 1 October 2026 is reported by two sources, not yet confirmed by the tax authority.
