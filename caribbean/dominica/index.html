Starting a business in Dominica: taxes, registration and steps

A plain-words guide for first-time entrepreneurs in Dominica: what you register, what you pay and when. Figures are 2026 estimates for planning. Inland Revenue Division (IRD) gives you the exact amounts when you register.

Steps to start

  1. Register with the Inland Revenue and get your tax number (TIN)
    A number that identifies you to the tax office. You need it for almost every other step.
  2. Choose your business name and check that it is free
    If you trade under any name other than your own, you must register it. Search the name at the CIPO first.
  3. Register the business name, or incorporate the company, with the Companies and Intellectual Property Office (CIPO)
    A sole trader registers the business name. A company must be incorporated first.
  4. Register with the Dominica Social Security
    Self-employed people must register and contribute. It gives access to a pension and other benefits.
  5. Open a separate bank account for the business
    Keeping business money apart from household money makes your numbers clearer and your taxes easier to pay.
  6. Register as an employer and prepare monthly payments (if you hire employees)
    With employees you pay social security (and income tax withheld, where it applies) every month. The authorities give you your payment dates when you register as an employer.
  7. Register for VAT (when your sales reach the threshold)
    Required once your yearly sales reach the VAT threshold (EC$300,000). Register with the Inland Revenue.

Choosing a legal form

  • Registered business name: You run the business as an individual under your own name or a business name. You and the business are the same person for tax. Usually the simplest way to start. The registry will quote you the current registration fee.
  • Limited company: The company is its own legal person, separate from you. It costs more to set up and has more paperwork, but it can limit your personal risk. A company costs more to set up than a business name. The registry will quote you the current fees.

Taxes and contributions

  • Income tax: 15% from EC$30,000 to EC$50,000, 25% up to EC$80,000, then 35% (the first EC$30,000 of income is not taxed).
  • Social Security (13.75%): Social security gives access to a pension and other benefits. Self-employed people pay about 13.75% of their declared earnings, up to EC$7,000 a month. The rate rises by 0.25 point each year until 2031.
  • Value Added Tax (VAT): 15%. Plan to register once your yearly sales reach this level. The IRD gives you the exact level when you register. Your filing period is set when you register.

Tax calendar

  • Payment dates: Your advance tax payments follow the schedule the Inland Revenue gives you when you register.
  • Each year: File your annual income tax return. The Inland Revenue Division sets the deadline each year.
  • Every month: Every month: pay social security (and income tax withheld, where it applies) for the previous month.

Frequently asked questions

How much tax does a self-employed person pay in Dominica?

On profit: 15% from EC$30,000 to EC$50,000, 25% up to EC$80,000, then 35% (the first EC$30,000 of income is not taxed). Contributions: Social Security 13.75% (up to EC$7,000 a month). Example: with EC$12,000 of sales a month, about EC$4,160 a month is left after tax.

Do I need to register for VAT in Dominica?

Value Added Tax (VAT) 15%; register from EC$300,000 in yearly sales. Plan to register once your yearly sales reach this level. The IRD gives you the exact level when you register. Your filing period is set when you register.

Where do I register a business in Dominica?

With Companies and Intellectual Property Office (CIPO) and Inland Revenue Division (IRD). How much does it cost to employ someone in Dominica?

On top of gross pay, the employer adds: Social Security 7.75%. Every month: pay social security (and income tax withheld, where it applies) for the previous month.
