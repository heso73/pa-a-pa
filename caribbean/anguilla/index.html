Starting a business in Anguilla: taxes, registration and steps

A plain-words guide for first-time entrepreneurs in Anguilla: what you register, what you pay and when. Figures are 2026 estimates for planning. Inland Revenue Department (IRD) gives you the exact amounts when you register.

Steps to start

  1. Choose your business name and check that it is free
    Search the name first.
  2. Apply for your business licence
    Fill in Form A for the Ministry of Economic Development. When it is approved, register and pay at the Inland Revenue Department. The fee depends on the activity.
  3. Register with the Social Security Board
    Self-employed people must register and contribute. The Interim Stabilisation Levy is filed with the Inland Revenue Department.
  4. Open a separate bank account for the business
    Keeping business money apart from household money makes your numbers clearer and your taxes easier to pay.
  5. Register as an employer and prepare payroll payments (if you hire employees)
    With employees you pay Social Security and the Interim Stabilisation Levy every month.
  6. Register for GST (when your sales reach the threshold)
    Required when your taxable sales reach EC$300,000 in twelve months, or when you expect them to. Apply within 15 days.

Choosing a legal form

  • Sole trader (registered business): You run the business as an individual under your own name or a business name. You and the business are the same person for tax. Usually the simplest way to start. You apply for a business licence (Form A) to the Ministry of Economic Development, then register and pay at the Inland Revenue Department. The fee depends on your activity.
  • Limited company: The company is its own legal person, separate from you. It costs more to set up and has more paperwork, but it can limit your personal risk. A company costs more to set up than a sole trader business. The Commercial Registry quotes the current fees.

Taxes and contributions

  • Social Security (8%): Social Security gives access to a pension and other benefits. Self-employed people pay 8% of their income, up to EC$7,000 a month.
  • Interim Stabilisation Levy (6%): A levy on earnings: self-employed people pay 6% on the part of their earnings above EC$2,000 a month.
  • Goods and Services Tax (GST): 13%. Register within 15 days of reaching the threshold, or if you expect to reach it within twelve months. Short-term accommodation, auctioneers and public entertainment providers register from their first day. GST returns are filed every month, by the 20th of the following month.

Anguilla has no income tax and no corporate tax. Your main costs are Social Security, the Interim Stabilisation Levy and, above EC$300,000 in sales, the 13% GST.

Tax calendar

  • Every month: If registered for GST: file your return by the 20th of the following month. With employees: pay Social Security and the Interim Stabilisation Levy.
  • Every year: Renew your business licence with the Inland Revenue Department.
  • Every month: Every month: pay Social Security and the Interim Stabilisation Levy for the previous month.

Frequently asked questions

How much tax does a self-employed person pay in Anguilla?

On profit: No personal income tax in Anguilla. Contributions: Social Security 8% (up to EC$7,000 a month), Interim Stabilisation Levy 6%. Example: with EC$12,000 of sales a month, about EC$4,850 a month is left after tax.

Do I need to register for GST in Anguilla?

Goods and Services Tax (GST) 13%; register from EC$300,000 in yearly sales. Register within 15 days of reaching the threshold, or if you expect to reach it within twelve months. Short-term accommodation, auctioneers and public entertainment providers register from their first day. GST returns are filed every month, by the 20th of the following month.

Where do I register a business in Anguilla?

With Anguilla Commercial Registry (ACORIS) and Inland Revenue Department (IRD). First steps: Choose your business name and check that it is free; Apply for your business licence; Register with the Social Security Board.

How much does it cost to employ someone in Anguilla?

On top of gross pay, the employer adds: Social Security 5.75%, Interim Stabilisation Levy 3%. Every month: pay Social Security and the Interim Stabilisation Levy for the previous month.
