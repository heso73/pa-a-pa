Starting a business in Aruba: taxes, registration and steps

A plain-words guide for first-time entrepreneurs in Aruba: what you register, what you pay and when. Figures are 2026 estimates for planning. Tax Department (Departamento di Impuesto) gives you the exact amounts when you register.

Steps to start

  1. Choose your business name and check that it is free
    Search the name at the Chamber of Commerce first.
  2. Register with the Chamber of Commerce
    Every business registers in the commercial register. A company is incorporated through a notary.
  3. Apply for your business licence
    Most activities need a licence. DEACI explains the steps and the documents.
  4. Register with the Tax Department
    You get your tax number and your turnover tax (BBO) account. File your returns even in months without sales.
  5. Register with the Social Insurance Bank (SVb)
    Self-employed people pay old age and widow/orphan insurance (AOV/AWW) on their own income.
  6. Open a separate bank account for the business
    Keeping business money apart from household money makes your numbers clearer and your taxes easier to pay.
  7. Register your employees and prepare payroll payments (if you hire employees)
    With employees you pay wage tax, AOV/AWW and AZV health insurance each month.

Choosing a legal form

  • Sole trader (registered business): You run the business as an individual under your own name or a business name. You and the business are the same person for tax. Usually the simplest way to start. The Chamber of Commerce quotes the registration fee. You also need a business licence for your activity.
  • Limited company: The company is its own legal person, separate from you. It costs more to set up and has more paperwork, but it can limit your personal risk. A company (NV or VBA) costs more to set up than a sole proprietorship. The Chamber of Commerce quotes the current fees.

Taxes and contributions

  • Income tax: 0% on the first Afl. 34,930 after the tax-free amount, 21% up to Afl. 63,904, 42% up to Afl. 135,527, then 52% (tax-free amount per year).
  • Old age and widow/orphan insurance (AOV/AWW) (13.5%): A pension insurance. Self-employed people pay 13.5% of their income, up to Afl. 85,000 a year. The Social Insurance Bank also tells you which health insurance premium applies to you.
  • Turnover tax (BBO, BAZV and BAVP): charged on sales, not on profit.

Aruba has no VAT. Businesses pay a turnover tax (BBO, BAZV and BAVP together: 7%) on their sales. The first Afl. 50,000 of yearly sales is exempt for small entrepreneurs.

Your yearly income tax is calculated after the Afl. 30,000 tax-free amount.

Tax calendar

  • Every month: File and pay your turnover tax (BBO) for the previous period. The Tax Department gives you the filing dates when you register.
  • Every year: File your income tax return.
  • Every month: Every month: pay wage tax, AOV/AWW and AZV for the previous month.

Frequently asked questions

How much tax does a self-employed person pay in Aruba?

On profit: 0% on the first Afl. 34,930 after the tax-free amount, 21% up to Afl. 63,904, 42% up to Afl. 135,527, then 52% (tax-free amount per year). Contributions: Old age and widow/orphan insurance (AOV/AWW) 13.5% (up to Afl. 7,083 a month). On sales: Turnover tax (BBO, BAZV and BAVP) 7%. Example: with Afl. 14,000 of sales a month, about Afl. 4,705 a month is left after tax.

Is there a sales tax or VAT in Aruba?

No. Aruba has no general sales tax. The costs to plan for are the ones in the taxes and contributions list on this page.

Where do I register a business in Aruba?

With Aruba Chamber of Commerce and Tax Department (Departamento di Impuesto). First steps: Choose your business name and check that it is free; Register with the Chamber of Commerce; Apply for your business licence.

How much does it cost to employ someone in Aruba?

On top of gross pay, the employer adds: Old age and widow/orphan insurance (AOV/AWW) 10.5%, General health insurance (AZV) 8.9%. Every month: pay wage tax, AOV/AWW and AZV for the previous month.
