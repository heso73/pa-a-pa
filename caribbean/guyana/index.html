Starting a business in Guyana: taxes, registration and steps

A plain-words guide for first-time entrepreneurs in Guyana: what you register, what you pay and when. Figures are 2026 estimates for planning. Guyana Revenue Authority (GRA) gives you the exact amounts when you register.

Steps to start

  1. Get your TIN (Taxpayer Identification Number) from GRA
    A number that identifies you to the tax office. You need it for almost every other step.
  2. Choose your business name and check that it is free
    If you trade under any name other than your own, you must register it under the Business Names (Registration) Act. Search the name at DCRA first.
  3. Register the business name, or incorporate the company, at DCRA
    You file the form and pay the fee at the DCRA office. A company must also meet the Companies Act requirements.
  4. Register with GRA and plan your advance tax payments
    Self-employed people pay income tax in advance, each quarter, and file a yearly return by April 30.
  5. Register with the National Insurance Scheme (NIS)
    Self-employed people must register and contribute. It gives access to a pension and other benefits.
  6. Open a separate bank account for the business
    Keeping business money apart from household money makes your numbers clearer and your taxes easier to pay.
  7. Register as an employer and prepare monthly payments (if you hire employees)
    With employees you pay income tax (PAYE) and NIS each month, by the 15th of the following month.
  8. Register for VAT (when your sales reach the threshold)
    Required when your yearly sales reach G$15,000,000. Apply to the GRA VAT department.

Choosing a legal form

  • Registered business name: You run the business as an individual under your own name or a business name. You and the business are the same person for tax. Usually the simplest way to start. The fee is about G$5,000.
  • Limited company: The company is its own legal person, separate from you. It costs more to set up and has more paperwork, but it can limit your personal risk. A company costs more to set up than a business name. The registry will quote you the current fees.

Taxes and contributions

  • Income tax: 25% on chargeable income up to G$3,360,000, then 35% (G$1,680,000 a year, or one third of your profit if that is more).
  • NIS (12.5%): National Insurance Scheme. It gives access to a pension and other benefits. Self-employed people pay 12.5% of their declared income, up to G$280,000 a month.
  • Value Added Tax (VAT): 14%. Apply to the GRA VAT department. VAT returns are due within 21 days after each period.

Optional deductions (for children, or for medical and life insurance premiums) are not included in your estimate. They would lower your tax.

Tax calendar

  • March 15, June 15, September 15 and December 15: Pay your advance income tax instalment.
  • By April 30: File your income tax return for the year before.
  • Every month: Every month: pay income tax (PAYE) and NIS for the previous month.

Frequently asked questions

How much tax does a self-employed person pay in Guyana?

On profit: 25% on chargeable income up to G$3,360,000, then 35% (G$1,680,000 a year, or one third of your profit if that is more). Contributions: NIS 12.5% (up to G$280,000 a month). Example: with G$800,000 of sales a month, about G$285,000 a month is left after tax.

Do I need to register for VAT in Guyana?

Value Added Tax (VAT) 14%; register from G$15,000,000 in yearly sales. Apply to the GRA VAT department. VAT returns are due within 21 days after each period.

Where do I register a business in Guyana?

With Deeds and Commercial Registries Authority (DCRA) and Guyana Revenue Authority (GRA). First steps: Get your TIN (Taxpayer Identification Number) from GRA; Choose your business name and check that it is free; Register the business name, or incorporate the company, at DCRA.

How much does it cost to employ someone in Guyana?

On top of gross pay, the employer adds: NIS 8.4%. Every month: pay income tax (PAYE) and NIS for the previous month.
