Starting a business in Haiti: taxes, registration and steps

A plain-words guide for first-time entrepreneurs in Haiti: what you register, what you pay and when. Figures are 2026 estimates for planning. Tax Authority (Direction Générale des Impôts, DGI) gives you the exact amounts when you register.

Steps to start

  1. Choose your business name and check that it is free
    Search the name at the Commercial Registry first.
  2. Register with the Commercial Registry
    Documents are filed in French or Creole. A company is incorporated through a notary.
  3. Get your tax number (NIF) from the DGI
    You also register for the turnover tax (TCA) if you sell goods or services.
  4. Pay your patente and get your professional card
    The patente is a yearly local tax with a fixed part and a part based on sales.
  5. Open a separate bank account for the business
    Keeping business money apart from household money makes your numbers clearer and your taxes easier to pay.
  6. Register with the ONA and OFATMA and prepare monthly payments (if you hire employees)
    With employees you pay the payroll tax (2%) by the 15th of each month, plus social security contributions to the ONA.

Choosing a legal form

  • Sole trader (registered business): You run the business as an individual under your own name or a business name. You and the business are the same person for tax. Usually the simplest way to start. You pay a yearly patente: a fixed fee of G 5,000, G 2,500 or G 1,250 depending on the town, plus 0.4% of your sales minus your payroll.
  • Limited company: The company is its own legal person, separate from you. It costs more to set up and has more paperwork, but it can limit your personal risk. A company is incorporated through a notary and costs more to set up than a sole trader business.

Taxes and contributions

  • Income tax: 0% up to G 60,000, 10% up to G 240,000, 15% up to G 480,000, 25% up to G 1,000,000, then 30% (no tax on the first G 60,000 a year).
  • Patente (proportional part): charged on sales, not on profit.
  • Turnover tax (TCA) (TCA): 10%. Register with the DGI when you start selling goods or services. Basic food and medicines are exempt. You file your TCA return every month.

Haiti has a 10% turnover tax (TCA). Sole traders pay income tax at 10% to 30% above G 60,000, and companies pay 30%.

The patente is a yearly local tax: a fixed fee (G 1,250 to G 5,000 depending on the town) plus 0.4% of sales minus payroll. Your estimate includes the 0.4% part on sales.

Tax calendar

  • Every month: File and pay your TCA return for the previous month.
  • From October 1 to January 31: File your final income tax return for the previous year.
  • Every month: Every month, from the 1st to the 15th: pay the payroll tax (2%) and the ONA contributions for the previous month.

Frequently asked questions

How much tax does a self-employed person pay in Haiti?

On profit: 0% up to G 60,000, 10% up to G 240,000, 15% up to G 480,000, 25% up to G 1,000,000, then 30% (no tax on the first G 60,000 a year). Contributions: None required for the owner. On sales: Patente (proportional part) 0.4%. Example: with G 150,000 of sales a month, about G 46,150 a month is left after tax.

Do I need to register for TCA in Haiti?

Turnover tax (TCA) (TCA) 10%; from the first sale. Register with the DGI when you start selling goods or services. Basic food and medicines are exempt. You file your TCA return every month.

Where do I register a business in Haiti?

With Commercial Registry (Registre du Commerce) and Tax Authority (Direction Générale des Impôts, DGI). First steps: Choose your business name and check that it is free; Register with the Commercial Registry; Get your tax number (NIF) from the DGI.

How much does it cost to employ someone in Haiti?

On top of gross pay, the employer adds: Payroll tax (TMS) 2%, Old age insurance (ONA) 6%, Work injury and health (OFATMA) 2%. Every month, from the 1st to the 15th: pay the payroll tax (2%) and the ONA contributions for the previous month.
