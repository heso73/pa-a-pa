Starting a business in Turks and Caicos Islands: taxes, registration and steps

A plain-words guide for first-time entrepreneurs in Turks and Caicos Islands: what you register, what you pay and when. Figures are 2026 estimates for planning. Business Licensing (Turks and Caicos Islands Government) gives you the exact amounts when you register.

Steps to start

  1. Choose your business name and check that it is free
    Search the name first.
  2. Get your business licence
    Every business needs one. The yearly fee runs from US$150 to US$7,500 depending on the activity, and the licence expires on March 31 each year.
  3. Register with the National Insurance Board (NIB) and the health insurance plan (NHIP)
    Self-employed people choose an income band for their contributions.
  4. Open a separate bank account for the business
    Keeping business money apart from household money makes your numbers clearer and your taxes easier to pay.
  5. Register as an employer with the NIB (if you hire employees)
    With employees you pay National Insurance and health insurance each month.

Choosing a legal form

  • Sole trader (registered business): You run the business as an individual under your own name or a business name. You and the business are the same person for tax. Usually the simplest way to start. Every self-employed person needs a business licence. The yearly fee runs from US$150 to US$7,500 depending on the occupation.
  • Limited company: The company is its own legal person, separate from you. It costs more to set up and has more paperwork, but it can limit your personal risk. A company costs more to set up than a sole trader business. The Companies Registry quotes the current fees.

Taxes and contributions

  • National Insurance (NIB) (11%): National Insurance gives access to a pension and other benefits. Self-employed people choose an income band, up to US$3,700 a month, and pay 11% of it.
  • National Health Insurance Plan (NHIP): A flat US$250 a month for the self-employed, which can be lower if you can prove income under US$3,000 a month.

The Turks and Caicos Islands has no income tax, no corporate tax and no VAT. The costs to plan for are your yearly business licence, your NIB and NHIP contributions and import duty (about 35%) on most goods you bring in.

A 12% tax applies to tourism accommodation.

Tax calendar

  • Every month: With employees: pay NHIP by the 14th and NIB by the 15th of the following month.
  • By March 31: Renew your business licence (renewals run from February to the end of April).
  • Every month: Every month: pay NHIP by the 14th and NIB by the 15th for the previous month.

Frequently asked questions

How much tax does a self-employed person pay in Turks and Caicos Islands?

On profit: No personal income tax in Turks and Caicos Islands. Contributions: National Insurance (NIB) 11% (up to US$3,700 a month), National Health Insurance Plan (NHIP). Example: with US$8,000 of sales a month, about US$2,865 a month is left after tax.

Is there a sales tax or VAT in Turks and Caicos Islands?

No. Turks and Caicos Islands has no general sales tax. The costs to plan for are the ones in the taxes and contributions list on this page.

Where do I register a business in Turks and Caicos Islands?

With Companies Registry (Financial Services Commission) and Business Licensing (Turks and Caicos Islands Government). First steps: Choose your business name and check that it is free; Get your business licence; Register with the National Insurance Board (NIB) and the health insurance plan (NHIP).

How much does it cost to employ someone in Turks and Caicos Islands?

On top of gross pay, the employer adds: National Insurance (NIB) 4.6%, Health insurance (NHIP) 3%. Every month: pay NHIP by the 14th and NIB by the 15th for the previous month.
