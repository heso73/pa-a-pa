Starting a business in US Virgin Islands: taxes, registration and steps

A plain-words guide for first-time entrepreneurs in US Virgin Islands: what you register, what you pay and when. Figures are 2026 estimates for planning. Virgin Islands Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) gives you the exact amounts when you register.

Steps to start

  1. Choose your business name and check that it is free
    Search the name first. A company registers its name with the Division of Corporations and Trademarks.
  2. Get your tax identification number
    You need an EIN or taxpayer number for the Bureau of Internal Revenue.
  3. Apply for your business licence
    Every business needs a licence from the Department of Licensing and Consumer Affairs before it starts trading.
  4. Register for the gross receipts tax
    Every business pays a 5% gross receipts tax once its receipts reach US$225,000 a year. You report it to the Bureau of Internal Revenue.
  5. Open a separate bank account for the business
    Keeping business money apart from household money makes your numbers clearer and your taxes easier to pay.
  6. Register as an employer (if you hire employees)
    With employees you withhold income tax and pay Social Security and Medicare on their wages, and register for territorial unemployment insurance.

Choosing a legal form

  • Sole proprietorship: You work in your own name. It is the simplest and cheapest way to start. The DLCA quotes the business licence fee for your activity.
  • Corporation or LLC: A company is a separate entity from you. It costs more to set up and needs more bookkeeping. A company is registered with the Division of Corporations and Trademarks, then licensed by the DLCA. The Lieutenant Governor's office quotes the filing fees.

Taxes and contributions

  • Income tax (paid to the Virgin Islands): The Virgin Islands copies the US federal tax code: 10% up to US$12,400 of taxable income, 12% up to US$50,400, 22% up to US$105,700, 24% up to US$201,775, 32% up to US$256,225, 35% up to US$640,600, then 37% (standard deduction).
  • Social Security (self-employment tax) (11.45%): Self-employed people pay both the employee and employer share: 12.4% on 92.35% of their earnings, up to US$184,500 of earnings a year. Half of it lowers your taxable income.
  • Medicare (self-employment tax) (2.68%): 2.9% on 92.35% of your earnings, with no ceiling. Half of it lowers your taxable income.
  • Gross receipts tax: charged on sales, not on profit.

The US Virgin Islands has no sales tax. Businesses pay a 5% gross receipts tax on everything they sell once their receipts reach US$225,000 a year, with no deduction for what they buy.

Income tax follows the US federal tax code and rates but is paid to the Virgin Islands Bureau of Internal Revenue, not the IRS. Self-employment tax is on top. Businesses approved by the Economic Development Commission can get large tax reductions: ask the Commission.

A corporation pays the federal rate of 21% plus a 10% territorial surtax (23.1% in total). The owner's own pay is not in the estimate.

Tax calendar

  • Every month: If your receipts are above US$225,000 a year, file and pay the gross receipts tax monthly (Form 720-VI) to the Bureau of Internal Revenue.
  • By April 15: File your income tax return with the Virgin Islands Bureau of Internal Revenue. Below US$225,000 of receipts, file the annual gross receipts return (Form 720-B) by January 30.
  • Every month: With employees: deposit the withheld income tax, Social Security and Medicare, and file the quarterly employer return (Form 941).

Frequently asked questions

How much tax does a self-employed person pay in US Virgin Islands?

On profit: The Virgin Islands copies the US federal tax code: 10% up to US$12,400 of taxable income, 12% up to US$50,400, 22% up to US$105,700, 24% up to US$201,775, 32% up to US$256,225, 35% up to US$640,600, then 37% (standard deduction). Contributions: Social Security (self-employment tax) 11.45% (up to US$16,648 a month), Medicare (self-employment tax) 2.68%. On sales: Gross receipts tax. Example: with US$9,000 of sales a month, about US$2,946 a month is left after tax.

Is there a sales tax or VAT in US Virgin Islands?

No. US Virgin Islands has no general sales tax. The costs to plan for are the ones in the taxes and contributions list on this page.

Where do I register a business in US Virgin Islands?

With Department of Licensing and Consumer Affairs (DLCA) and Virgin Islands Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR). First steps: Choose your business name and check that it is free; Get your tax identification number; Apply for your business licence.

How much does it cost to employ someone in US Virgin Islands?

On top of gross pay, the employer adds: Social Security (employer share) 6.2%, Medicare (employer share) 1.45%. With employees: deposit the withheld income tax, Social Security and Medicare, and file the quarterly employer return (Form 941).
