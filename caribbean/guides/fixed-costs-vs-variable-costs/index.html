Fixed costs vs variable costs: why the difference matters

Not every cost behaves the same way when sales go up or down. Knowing which is which is the first step to knowing how much you must sell.

In short
  • Fixed costs do not move with sales; variable costs follow sales.
  • Cutting a fixed cost helps every month; cutting a variable cost helps every sale.
  • These two figures are enough to calculate your break-even point.

Fixed costs

They stay the same whether you sell a lot or nothing: rent, insurance, subscriptions, loan repayments, an accountant, and your own pay if you set it.

Variable costs

They rise when you sell more: materials or goods, packaging, delivery, platform commissions, payment fees and, in many cases, taxes charged on sales.

An example: a candle maker

She sells each candle for 20. Materials and packaging cost 6. The online marketplace takes 10%, which is 2. That leaves 12 per candle to pay the fixed costs (before income tax, to keep it simple).

Selling price20
Materials and packaging− 6
Marketplace commission (10%)− 2
Left per candle12

Her fixed costs are 600 a month (shared workshop, insurance, subscriptions). She must sell 600 ÷ 12 = 50 candles a month just to break even.

Why the difference changes everything

Raising fixed costs from 600 to 900 lifts the break-even point from 50 to 75 candles. Cutting materials from 6 to 5 raises what is left per candle to 13 and lowers break-even to 47 candles. Acting on the right kind of cost avoids wasted effort.

Common mistakes

  • Counting your own time as zero: it is neither fixed nor variable, it is pay you must plan for.
  • Putting a variable cost in the fixed column, or the reverse: break-even comes out wrong.
  • Forgetting payment fees and taxes on sales.
  • Signing a long lease or contract before you have customers.

Frequently asked questions

Is rent a fixed or variable cost?

Usually fixed: it does not depend on your sales. A rent set as a percentage of sales would be variable.

How do I lower my fixed costs?

Share a workspace or work from home if your activity allows it, buy second-hand equipment, cancel unused subscriptions and avoid long contracts.

How do I calculate my break-even point?

Divide your fixed costs by what is left from each sale after variable costs. The free test does it for you with your country's rules.
