Income tax threshold in Trinidad and Tobago in 2026

The personal allowance in Trinidad and Tobago is TT$90,000 a year, which is TT$7,500 a month. Below it you pay no PAYE income tax. Here is the pay at which tax starts, the rates above it, and a calculator.

In short
  • Tax-free allowance: TT$90,000 a year (TT$7,500 a month, about TT$1,731 a week).
  • Income tax: 25% on chargeable income up to TT$1,000,000 a year, then 30%.
  • Employee National Insurance (5.4% in 2026) is taken off first, so income tax starts at about TT$7,928 a month of gross pay.

What the threshold is

Your chargeable income is your yearly pay minus employee National Insurance minus the TT$90,000 allowance. If the result is zero or negative, no income tax is due. National Insurance (employee share 5.4% in 2026, up to TT$13,600 of pay a month) and the Health Surcharge still apply even when income tax does not.

What changes in 2027

National Insurance rises to 19.2% in total (employee 6.4%) from 4 January 2027, which lowers take-home pay slightly and moves the pay at which income tax starts. The 2027 Budget kept the TT$90,000 allowance for most people.

Frequently asked questions

What is the income tax threshold in Trinidad and Tobago?

TT$90,000 a year, which is TT$7,500 a month. Chargeable income below it is not taxed.

What is the minimum salary for PAYE in Trinidad and Tobago?

There is no minimum salary as such: income tax starts once chargeable income passes the TT$90,000 allowance, which is about TT$7,928 a month of gross pay after employee National Insurance.

What is the income tax rate in Trinidad and Tobago?

25% on chargeable income up to TT$1,000,000 a year and 30% above.

Does a minimum-wage worker pay income tax?

No. The yearly pay at the minimum wage is about TT$42,640, below the TT$90,000 allowance.
