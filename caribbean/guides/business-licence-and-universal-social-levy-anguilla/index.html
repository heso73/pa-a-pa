Business Licence and Universal Social Levy in Anguilla

In Anguilla, you apply for a business licence through the Ministry of Finance, and the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) issues the certificate.

In short
  • Persons operating a business must apply to the Ministry of Finance for a business licence.
  • Once approved, the fee is paid to the IRD, which issues a business certificate to display at the business.
  • Licences are valid until 31 December of the year received and are renewed annually at the IRD.
  • The IRD lists a Universal Social Levy (formerly the Interim Stabilisation Levy) of 3%, plus accommodation tax, liquor licences and property tax.

Business licence

According to the Government of Anguilla, anyone operating a business applies to the Ministry of Finance. Fees vary with the nature of the business and factors such as stock value. After approval, the fee is paid to the IRD, which issues the business certificate you must display in your place of business.

Annual renewal

All licences are valid until 31 December of the year received and must be renewed annually at the Inland Revenue Department.

Universal Social Levy

The IRD describes the Universal Social Levy, formerly the Interim Stabilisation Levy, as a 3% levy. The IRD publishes monthly due dates: ask the department how it applies to you as an employer or a self-employed person.

What to confirm

We could not confirm on the pages found whether a sales tax threshold applies to small businesses. Ask the IRD before you set your prices.

Information verified on 21 November 2026 on Government of Anguilla pages: Inland Revenue Department (ird.gov.ai) and the Business and Taxes service pages (gov.ai).

Frequently asked questions

Who gives the business licence in Anguilla?

You apply to the Ministry of Finance, and the IRD issues the certificate after the fee is paid.

How long is the licence valid?

Until 31 December of the year received, then it is renewed annually at the IRD.

What is the Universal Social Levy?

A 3% levy, formerly the Interim Stabilisation Levy, administered by the IRD.

Does the licence fee depend on the business?

Yes: fees vary by the nature of the business and factors such as stock value.
