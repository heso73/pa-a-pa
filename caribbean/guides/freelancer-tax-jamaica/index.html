Freelancer and content creator taxes in Jamaica

Freelancers, designers, photographers, developers and content creators are treated as self-employed in Jamaica. Being self-employed does not exempt you from paying taxes: you are solely responsible for paying them to Tax Administration Jamaica (TAJ).

In short
  • You pay income tax on profit above the tax-free amount, plus NIS, NHT and Education Tax.
  • Payments are quarterly: March 15, June 15, September 15 and December 15.
  • Keep a record of every payment received and every business cost.

What you pay

The same four amounts as any self-employed person. Try the calculator with your expected yearly profit. Up to about J$2 million of profit you pay almost no income tax, but contributions still apply.

What to do first

  • Get a TRN from TAJ.
  • Register a business name at the Companies Office of Jamaica if you invoice under a business name (see the cost).
  • Register as a self-employed contributor for NHT and NIS.
  • Put the quarterly dates in your calendar: see the tax deadlines.

Keep your records

Save invoices, receipts and bank statements. Your profit is sales minus business costs, so every cost you can prove lowers your taxable profit. Separate your business money from personal money from day one.

When GCT applies

General Consumption Tax is only required once your yearly taxable sales reach J$15,000,000. See GCT registration. Figures last checked on 10 November 2026 against the official sources (TAJ, NHT) and the PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries. Confirm the current amount with the authority before you pay.

Frequently asked questions

Do freelancers pay tax in Jamaica?

Yes. Freelancers are self-employed and pay income tax above the tax-free amount, plus NIS, NHT and Education Tax.

Do I have to register a company to freelance?

No. You can work as a sole trader. You need a TRN, and a registered business name if you trade under one.

When do freelancers pay tax in Jamaica?

Four times a year: March 15, June 15, September 15 and December 15.
