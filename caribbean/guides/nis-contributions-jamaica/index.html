NIS contributions in Jamaica: rates, ceiling and examples

The National Insurance Scheme (NIS) is the first deduction taken from pay in Jamaica. Here are the rates, the yearly ceiling and what it means for your take-home income.

In short
  • Employee 3%, employer 3%, self-employed 6%.
  • Contributions apply to earnings up to J$5,000,000 a year (J$416,667 a month).
  • The most a self-employed person pays in a year is 6% of J$5,000,000, which is J$300,000.
  • NIS is worked out first; it lowers the amount Education Tax and income tax are based on.

The rates

Employee3%
Employer3%
Self-employed6%

A self-employed person pays both shares, which is why the rate is 6%.

The ceiling

NIS is charged only on earnings up to J$5,000,000 a year. Above that, no more NIS is due. That caps a self-employed person at J$300,000 a year.

Worked examples

Employee earning J$120,000 a monthNIS J$3,600 a month
Self-employed with J$2,280,000 profitNIS J$136,800 a year
Self-employed with J$6,000,000 profitNIS J$300,000 a year (ceiling)

Why it matters for your other taxes

For an employee, NIS is subtracted from gross pay to give statutory income, the base for Education Tax. Income tax follows after that.

Frequently asked questions

What is the NIS rate in Jamaica?

3% for the employee, 3% for the employer and 6% for the self-employed.

Is there a maximum NIS contribution?

Yes. NIS applies to earnings up to J$5,000,000 a year, so the self-employed maximum is J$300,000 a year.

Do the self-employed pay NIS?

Yes, at 6%, through quarterly payments and the annual return.
