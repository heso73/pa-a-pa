How to start a business in the Caribbean: first steps in order

Many first-time owners begin with the end: a logo, a website, a company name. What decides success comes earlier: will customers pay, and do the numbers work? These steps put the cheap, reversible work first.

In short
  • Test the idea and work out the numbers before you register or spend.
  • Every country has its own registry, tax office and social security body: the country guides list them.
  • Some activities (food, transport, health) need a licence or permit before you sell.

1. Test the idea with real customers

Talk to five people or businesses who might buy. Ask what they pay for this today and what is missing. Look for a concrete commitment: a pre-order, a deposit, a firm date.

2. Work out the numbers

Calculate the cost of one product or job, the monthly sales you need to cover costs and pay yourself, and what you must keep aside for taxes. See fixed costs vs variable costs. The free Pa a Pa Caribbean test does this with your country's tax rules.

3. Choose a structure

Most countries offer two main options: trading under a registered business name, or forming a limited company. The first is simpler and cheaper; the second can limit your personal risk but brings more paperwork and costs. Ask the company registry or an accountant before you decide.

4. Register and get your tax numbers

Register the business name or company with the official registry, get a tax number from the tax office, and register for social contributions if you work for yourself or hire people. Register for sales tax if your sales reach the threshold. Ask whether your activity needs a licence or permit.

5. Open a separate bank account

Run every sale and cost through it, and pay yourself a fixed amount each month. Keep a second small account for taxes.

6. Consider insurance

Property and stock cover matters in hurricane areas; liability cover protects you if a customer is harmed or loses money because of you. Get two or three quotes and add the cost to your fixed costs.

7. Sell, then track

Compare your sales to your break-even point every month and your cash to your forecast. Avoid the most common mistakes.

Country guides

Taxes, contributions, sales tax and registration steps differ by country. Start with: Jamaica, Trinidad and Tobago, Guyana, Barbados, The Bahamas or Belize. The app covers 29 countries and territories.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to register my business before I sell?

In most places yes, and some activities also need a licence or permit. Check with the registry and the tax office of your country before you start selling.

How much money do I need to start a business?

It depends on the business. List what you must pay before the first sale (equipment, stock, insurance, a cash reserve) and compare it with your savings. The free test helps you estimate it.

Can I start while keeping my job?

Often yes, subject to your employment contract. It is a prudent way to test the business without losing your income.
