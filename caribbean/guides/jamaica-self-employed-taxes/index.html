Self-employed taxes in Jamaica: what you pay in 2026

If you work for yourself in Jamaica, you pay income tax on your profit plus three contributions: NIS, NHT and Education Tax. Here are the rates, the dates and a worked example, so you can plan before your first payment.

In short
  • Income tax: 25% on income from J$1,902,360 (the yearly tax-free amount) up to J$6,000,000, then 30%.
  • Contributions: NIS 6%, NHT 3%, Education Tax 2.25%.
  • You pay quarterly (March 15, June 15, September 15, December 15) and file your yearly income estimate (form S04A) by March 15.

The rates

  • Income tax: 25% on income from J$1,902,360 up to J$6,000,000, then 30%. The first J$1,902,360 a year is tax-free.
  • NIS (National Insurance Scheme), 6%: gives access to a pension and other benefits. It applies up to a yearly ceiling of J$5,000,000.
  • NHT (National Housing Trust), 3%: helps contributors get housing loans.
  • Education Tax, 2.25%: funds schools. It is calculated after the NIS amount is taken off.

The tax-free amount rose to J$1,902,360 on 1 April 2026 and is set to rise again on 1 April 2027, according to the Tax Administration Jamaica (TAJ) schedule. For 2026 as a whole, the effective tax-free amount is slightly lower because the rise starts in April.

A worked example

A sole trader sells J$400,000 a month. After the cost of goods and fixed costs, the profit before tax is J$2,280,000 for the year.

When you pay and file

  • March 15, June 15, September 15 and December 15: pay your self-employed statutory payments (income tax, NIS, NHT and Education Tax).
  • By March 15: file your income estimate for the year (form S04A). It sets the amount of your quarterly payments.

Planning tip

Set aside a share of each payment you receive in a separate account, so the quarterly dates do not catch you short. To see how much of your sales is really yours, work out your costs first: see fixed costs vs variable costs.

Frequently asked questions

How much tax does a self-employed person pay in Jamaica?

Income tax of 25% on income from J$1,902,360 up to J$6,000,000, then 30%, plus NIS 6%, NHT 3% and Education Tax 2.25%. In the example on this page, a profit of J$2,280,000 leaves about J$1,966,368 after all four.

When do self-employed people pay tax in Jamaica?

Four times a year: March 15, June 15, September 15 and December 15. The yearly income estimate (form S04A) is due by March 15.

Do I need a TRN to work for myself in Jamaica?

Yes. The Taxpayer Registration Number (TRN) identifies you to the tax office and is needed for almost every other registration step. You get it from Tax Administration Jamaica.
