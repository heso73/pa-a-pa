Starting a business in Jamaica: taxes, registration and steps

A plain-words guide for first-time entrepreneurs in Jamaica: what you register, what you pay and when. Figures are 2026 estimates for planning. Tax Administration Jamaica (TAJ) gives you the exact amounts when you register.

Steps to start

  1. Get your TRN (Taxpayer Registration Number) from TAJ
    A number that identifies you to the tax office. You need it for almost every other step.
  2. Choose your business name and check that it is free
    If you trade under any name other than your own full name, you must register it. Search the name at COJ first.
  3. Register with the Companies Office (COJ)
    Online or in person. For a business name, registration lasts three years, then you renew it.
  4. Register as self-employed with TAJ and file your yearly income estimate (form S04A) by March 15
    This estimate sets the amount of your quarterly payments to TAJ.
  5. Open a separate bank account for the business
    Keeping business money apart from household money makes your numbers clearer and your taxes easier to pay.
  6. Register as an employer and prepare monthly payroll payments (if you hire employees)
    With employees you pay deductions (income tax, NIS, NHT, Education Tax, HEART/NTA) to TAJ by the 14th of each month.
  7. Register for GCT (when your sales reach the threshold)
    Required when your yearly sales pass the GCT threshold. Register within 21 days of crossing it.

Choosing a legal form

  • Registered business name: You run the business as an individual under your own name or a business name. You and the business are the same person for tax. Usually the simplest way to start.
  • Limited company: The company is its own legal person, separate from you. It costs more to set up and has more paperwork, but it can limit your personal risk.

Taxes and contributions

  • Income tax: 25% on income from J$1,902,360 up to J$6,000,000, then 30% (tax-free amount per year).
  • NIS (6%): National Insurance Scheme. It gives access to a pension and other benefits.
  • NHT (3%): National Housing Trust. It helps contributors get housing loans.
  • Education Tax (2.25%): A contribution that funds schools.
  • General Consumption Tax, like VAT (GCT): 15%. Register within 21 days of crossing the threshold. Returns are due by the 25th of the month after the period.

Tax calendar

  • March 15, June 15, September 15 and December 15: Pay your self-employed statutory payments (income tax, NIS, NHT, Education Tax).
  • By March 15: File your income estimate for the year (form S04A). It sets the amount of your quarterly payments.
  • Every month: Every month: pay payroll deductions for the previous month (form S01).

Frequently asked questions

How much tax does a self-employed person pay in Jamaica?

On profit: 25% on income from J$1,902,360 up to J$6,000,000, then 30% (tax-free amount per year). Contributions: NIS 6% (up to J$416,667 a month), NHT 3%, Education Tax 2.25%. Example: with J$400,000 of sales a month, about J$163,864 a month is left after tax.

Do I need to register for GCT in Jamaica?

General Consumption Tax, like VAT (GCT) 15%; register from J$15,000,000 in yearly sales. Register within 21 days of crossing the threshold. Returns are due by the 25th of the month after the period.

Where do I register a business in Jamaica?

With Companies Office of Jamaica (COJ) and Tax Administration Jamaica (TAJ). First steps: Get your TRN (Taxpayer Registration Number) from TAJ; Choose your business name and check that it is free; Register with the Companies Office (COJ).

How much does it cost to employ someone in Jamaica?

On top of gross pay, the employer adds: NIS 3%, NHT 3%, Education Tax 3.5%, HEART/NTA 3%. Every month: pay payroll deductions for the previous month (form S01).
