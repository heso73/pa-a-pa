Starting a business in Suriname: taxes, registration and steps

A plain-words guide for first-time entrepreneurs in Suriname: what you register, what you pay and when. Figures are 2026 estimates for planning. Tax Administration (Belastingdienst Suriname) gives you the exact amounts when you register.

Steps to start

  1. Choose your business name and check that it is free
    Search the name at the Chamber of Commerce and Industry first.
  2. Register with the Chamber of Commerce and Industry (KKF)
    Every business registers in the commercial register. A company is incorporated through a notary.
  3. Register with the Tax Administration
    You get your tax number and register for VAT (BTW). File your returns even when you have no sales.
  4. Check the licence your activity needs
    Some activities, such as food, transport or health, need a permit from the ministry that supervises them.
  5. Open a separate bank account for the business
    Keeping business money apart from household money makes your numbers clearer and your taxes easier to pay.
  6. Register your employees and prepare monthly payments (if you hire employees)
    With employees you withhold wage tax and pay the social premiums each month.

Choosing a legal form

  • Sole trader (registered business): You run the business as an individual under your own name or a business name. You and the business are the same person for tax. Usually the simplest way to start. The Chamber of Commerce and Industry quotes the registration fee for a sole proprietorship.
  • Limited company: The company is its own legal person, separate from you. It costs more to set up and has more paperwork, but it can limit your personal risk. A company (NV or BV) is incorporated through a notary and costs more to set up than a sole proprietorship.

Taxes and contributions

  • Income tax: 0% up to SRD 3,000, 8% up to SRD 14,000, 18% up to SRD 24,000, 28% up to SRD 38,000, then 38% (no tax on the first SRD 3,000 a year).
  • Value Added Tax (BTW): 10%. Register with the Tax Administration when you start. The standard rate is 10%, with 5% on water, electricity and cooking gas, and 0% on exports. You file your BTW return every month.

Suriname has a 10% VAT (BTW) since January 2023. Sole proprietors pay income tax on their profit at 8% to 38%, and companies pay 36%.

The Tax Administration publishes the exact income tax thresholds each year, because the Surinamese dollar loses value quickly.

Frequently asked questions

How much tax does a self-employed person pay in Suriname?

On profit: 0% up to SRD 3,000, 8% up to SRD 14,000, 18% up to SRD 24,000, 28% up to SRD 38,000, then 38% (no tax on the first SRD 3,000 a year). Contributions: None required for the owner. Example: with SRD 60,000 of sales a month, about SRD 16,773 a month is left after tax.

Do I need to register for BTW in Suriname?

Value Added Tax (BTW) 10%; from the first sale. Register with the Tax Administration when you start. The standard rate is 10%, with 5% on water, electricity and cooking gas, and 0% on exports. You file your BTW return every month.

Where do I register a business in Suriname?

With Chamber of Commerce and Industry (KKF) and Tax Administration (Belastingdienst Suriname). First steps: Choose your business name and check that it is free; Register with the Chamber of Commerce and Industry (KKF); Register with the Tax Administration.
